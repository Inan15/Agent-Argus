## 3. Challenges & Growth Areas

### 3.1 🔴 Vacuous guards are this project's dominant defect class, and Epic 11 produced five instances in five stories

This is the finding of the epic, and it deserves to be stated without softening: **Argus is a tool whose entire purpose is detecting tests that pass without testing anything, and its own guard estate produced five instances of that defect class in a single epic.**

| # | Instance | Where the observable failed | Caught by |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **11.3's committed guard** was blind to `run: \| # comment` **and** `run: \|2` — **102 of 108 legal block headers**. An ordinary, non-adversarial YAML edit would have silently reopened `DF-9-2-D` with the suite green | The resolver classified any non-empty remainder as the command, so the indented body — where the script and the injection live — was never scanned | 🔴 **REVIEW** (iteration 1 FAIL) |
| 2 | **11.5's `TC-ArgusAgent-RELEASE-001-11`** walks the **source tree** with `ast` to assert a claim about the **distribution**. `import _registry` inside a function body is the same AST node as one at module level, so it **stayed green across the entire fix** and would have stayed green with the record left stale | The observable (source-tree import graph) cannot move when the defect (module-level vs lazy import) changes | 🟢 create-story (§A.2) |
| 3 | **11.4's rejected version assertion** — `assert tree-sitter < 0.26` would have been **vacuous by construction**: the demonstrated drift occurs at an **in-bound** version, so the pin is green on the exact tree where the false green is live | A version string never demonstrates that a wrong version changes parse behaviour | 🟢 create-story (§0.3) |
| 4 | **11.3's *existing* resolver** was already blind to single-line `run: cmd` — returning an **empty** line set for `- run: echo "${{ inputs.evil }}"`. The obvious guard, built by inheriting it, would have been vacuous on day one | Same as #1, one shape further in | 🟢 create-story (§A.3) |
| 5 | **11.2's guard was sound but `DF-8-2-B`'s PREMISE had expired.** The 2026-08-04 reasoning *"not a false green, because the grading stage misclassifies identically"* was true when Java had no grammar and false once it gained one | The reasoning's observable — stage agreement — stayed true while its *consequence* inverted | 🟢 create-story (§0.3) |

**Score it honestly: four of five were caught at create-story time by re-measurement, one at review, and none shipped.** That is a good outcome and it is not a sustainable control, because four of the five were caught by a *human-written analysis step* rather than by anything mechanical, and the one the analysis missed was caught only because a reviewer built a synthetic the author had not thought of.

**The pattern that worked, stated precisely enough to become a rule, because it recurs verbatim across all five instances:**

> **A guard is adequate only if (i) its OBSERVABLE is named, (ii) the defect has been demonstrated to move that observable — RED at the REAL seam, not against a reconstruction of it, and (iii) at least one adversarial variant is GENERATED from the grammar, registry or table the guard closes over, rather than hand-listed.**

Each clause is load-bearing and each is traceable to a specific failure above. (i) is what would have caught #2 and #3 at design time — *"can the defect exist while my observable is unchanged?"* answers both instantly. (ii) is what distinguishes 11.4's `-125` (disables the real predicate at the real call site) from a strawman. (iii) is precisely the difference between 11.3's first attempt (three shapes it thought of) and its fix (108 generated from the grammar) — and the reviewer's iteration-2 check then generated spellings outside even that set and found none blind.

**This is AI-E11-1, and it belongs in `architecture.md` §Enforcement as a story-authoring standard, not in a task list.** Epic 10's §4.6 established the ranking with evidence: rules land, stories get scheduled, human runbook steps do not.

## 4. Key Insights

**1. A guard's OBSERVABLE is the whole thing, and this project keeps writing guards whose observable cannot move when the defect does.** Five instances in one epic (§3.1), and they are all the same question asked in different vocabularies: *can the defect exist while my observable is unchanged?* Source tree vs distribution (11.5). Version string vs parse behaviour (11.4). Block-scalar-only resolver vs any `run:` body (11.3). Substring scan vs `ast.Call` (11.1's harness-agreement guard, where a naive scan reported a docstring and a comment as production sites that had cleared the precision gate). Stage-agreement vs its consequence (11.2's expired premise). **One question, asked at design time, catches all five.** That is why the recommendation is a story-authoring rule and not five fixes.

**2. The difference between a fix and a closure is whether the adversarial case was GENERATED or REMEMBERED.** 11.3 is the epic's clearest demonstration, because it shows both halves. Its first guard enumerated **three** shapes its author thought of, and an ordinary fourth defeated it. Its fix generated **108** from YAML's `c-b-block-header` grammar and re-keyed classification onto the grammar's own discriminator. The reviewer then generated spellings **outside** the dev's 108 — digit indicators the dev had not sampled, a comment glued to the indicator with no space, CRLF endings, a quoted `#` inside a command — and found none blind. **The list a careful author writes is always shorter than the space, and the only reliable way to find that out before a consumer does is to enumerate the space mechanically.**

**3. An append-only ledger preserves the record and preserves the error, and the project has not yet decided which a reader sees first.** §3.4 evidence immutability is correct and was honoured perfectly: `DF-8-2-B`'s expired premise, `DF-9-2-D`'s wrong coordinate and wrong count, and `DF-9-2-A`'s false close condition were all corrected in appended closure notes with the originals byte-intact. 11.3's dev put it best: correcting them in place *"would destroy the record of what the project believed when it filed the item."* **And a reader who opens `deferred-work.md`, finds `DF-9-2-D`, and stops reading has read a false coordinate.** Three of the epic's five premise failures were in ledger entries. This is not an argument for editing them — it is an argument for a forward pointer at the original entry, which costs one line and breaks no immutability rule.

**4. The most valuable thing 11.5 found was not the bug — it was that a guard had been publishing the claim that it was not vacuous.** `DF-9-2-A`'s close condition promised that updating `_NOT_IMPORTABLE_FROM_DISTRIBUTION` was safe *"because it is pinned in BOTH directions, so a fix that leaves the record stale goes RED."* It was not, it did not, and `README.md:166-167` **published that false claim verbatim to consumers.** The guard stayed green across the entire fix and it stayed green while the published figures rotted from *"66 of the 71"* to a measured 67 of 72 over two epics. **A vacuous guard is worse than no guard, because the project builds documentation on top of it and then publishes that documentation.** The correction was recorded in the ledger rather than quietly satisfied, which is the only acceptable handling.

**5. A blanket prohibition is a fence, not a ruling, and it works until the first story it cannot describe.** The operator's *"no Epic 11 story may create or stage a new `argus/**` file"* was a genuinely good call: measured satisfiable by every story before it started, zero halts, zero regenerations, all partition ids byte-unchanged. It also cost three recorded design compromises, and **it is logically inapplicable to the very next story in the plan** (§3.3). Fences buy time at a compounding price; the price is now 204 of 207 lines and a bootstrap circularity in the first story of the next epic.

**6. The "re-measure at create-story time" control is this project's single most productive investment, and its value is that it changes stories rather than correcting figures.** Five for five (§2.1). In 11.2 it turned a 🟢 latent ledger item into a release-blocking false green in the PRD-fatal direction. In 11.4 it disproved the epic's own headline premise and redirected the story from a version assertion — which would have been vacuous by construction — to a behavioural canary at the real loader seam. In 11.5 it prevented a **fabricated finding**: two of the three README falsehoods the epic named were already fixed, and re-fixing them would have manufactured work and a false record. **A control that changes what a story *is*, five times out of five, is not a checklist item. It is the method.**

**7. Cross-story fences and pins made the epic composable, and that is why five stories fit in one dirty tree without colliding.** 11.1's `DN-3` pinned `replay_harness` off every user-facing path and named it *"Story 11.5's wheel defect"* verbatim in a committed test — so when 11.5 made the module safe to import, the pin still correctly asserted it was not appropriate to import from `argus.cli`. 10.4's `-114` was written to *detect Story 11.4 crossing its fence*, and 11.4 narrowed it explicitly with its reason rather than deleting it. 11.5's `-56` will fail the day Story 12.7 ships a command asset without removing the FORTHCOMING marker, **and** the day someone removes the marker early. **These are deferrals addressed to triggers, and this epic produced more of them than any before it.**

**8. Everything the epic learned about publishing, it learned without publishing anything.** No push, no tag, no release, no dispatch, no index upload — across five stories whose entire subject was the published artifact, verified independently by every reviewer. 11.3 hardened an action it was forbidden to run; 11.5 measured a wheel it built into a temp directory outside the repository and then discarded. **The discipline held under the strongest possible temptation, which is the only condition under which discipline means anything.**
