## 2. What Went Well

**1. Gate-first sequencing worked, and it is measurable rather than asserted.** 10.1 was deliberately the control that would have caught 10.2–10.4. The standard it wrote — *a run id is sha-scoped; cite the run **and** the sha it covers, or record `NOT ESTABLISHED`* — then held **four consecutive times without further enforcement**, including in Story 10.4, the story with the strongest possible incentive to cite something (it was sitting on five red tests that a regeneration would have cleared). 10.4 refused to regenerate evidence artifacts at a sha that did not contain the deltas they describe. **The control caught the epic that built it.**

**2. Every load-bearing AC in the epic was a closure, not a list — and the closures kept catching the lists.** All five stories made the same design choice independently of each other (10.2 DN-4, 10.5 DN-2 state it explicitly). Twice the guard's own positive control caught the enumeration being wrong *during the story that was correcting it*: 10.2's spec-claim guard found a 12th site the hand list missed (the enumeration was about to be wrong a fourth time), and 10.5's guard exposed a half-struck-commitment hole in its own strike check.

**3. RED-first was discharged, re-discharged, and mechanised.** 10.1 ran its guard red against the uncorrected documents, then — because the test code changed afterwards — **repeated the red demonstration with the final code**, restoring all four documents byte-identically by sha256 round-trip. That is AI-E3-1 taken seriously rather than recited. 10.4's reviewer went further and injected a fifth unregistered `GrammarFailure` arm into the **real** loader to prove the closure guard bites, then restored the file and verified `git diff --quiet`.

**4. Reviews were executed, not read.** Iteration-1 PASS did not mean iteration-1 skim: the 10.3 reviewer fuzzed the Live-Key Safeguard over 133 hostile combinations and monkeypatched a rogue flag into the real `build_parser`; the 10.2 reviewer independently verified `Path.glob` includes dotfiles rather than trusting a docstring and partitioned every dirty path by mtime to prove the write-set fence held with zero leakage; the 10.4 reviewer verified the provenance chain by git forensics (`a9cc933` genuinely contains the deltas the artifacts cite, and is an ancestor of `HEAD`).

**5. Honest degradation was applied to the project's own governance, not just to the product.** `NOT ESTABLISHED` is now a first-class recordable state in `architecture.md` §H, explicitly modelled on `AUDIT_FAILED`-is-not-a-verdict and `INSUFFICIENT_COVERAGE`. The tool and its governance visibly share one principle, and the cross-reference is written into the rule.

**6. **7. Every new ledger entry has a named human.** Thirteen filings, thirteen owners. ## 3. Challenges & Growth Areas

### 3.1 🔴 No CI run has ever seen a line of Epic 10

Re-measured this session: `HEAD` = `93adc94`, **6 commits ahead of `origin/master`**, `git tag -l` **empty**. Every gate figure in every Epic 10 story is a **LOCAL** run on a Windows host at CPython 3.11.15; CI is ubuntu × 3.10/3.11/3.12. This is not a theoretical gap — the project has direct measured evidence that it matters: **six of the twelve commits in `cd60dbb..00c8d1b` were host-portability defects invisible on exactly this machine and fatal on the runner**, and that is precisely why 10.1's rule says a local run is *necessary and not sufficient*.

The epic handled this correctly at every step and never once claimed otherwise. The consequence is nonetheless real: **Epic 11's stated dependency is "Epic 10 — all five stories", and what it would actually be building on is a tree no gate has executed against.**

### 3.2 🟠 The `git add` requirement and the dogfood staleness guards are in direct mechanical conflict

This halted Story 10.4 and it is the epic's most consequential structural finding. `enumerate_minions_source_files` reads **`git ls-files`**, which reports the **index**, not `HEAD`. `AI-E8-1` (and every story frontmatter since) requires the dev to `git add` new files, *"because `git diff` cannot see an untracked path"*. Therefore **staging one new `argus/` module moves the audited population** (71 → 72), which moves total LOC, the budget `$X`, the critical-set size, and — via NFR-SC1 bin-packing — the `partition_id` sha256s that three committed evidence artifacts record.

Measured by the dev: before the mandated `git add`, **one** test was red; after it, **five**. Neither half can be undone by the story — un-staging re-opens AI-E8-1, and shrinking source comments to hold a sha256 stable is tail-wagging-dog. Filed as **`DF-10-4-D`**, owner Engineering Lead, `target_story: 12-1`.

**The trigger is "any story that stages an `argus/**` source file"** — strictly stronger than `DF-8-5-B`'s recorded *"any commit that changes `argus/**` composition"*, and the entry says so. §5 of this document explains why the target matters more than usual.

### 3.3 🟠 Four stories were implemented against one uncommitted working tree

10.1, 10.2, 10.3 and 10.4 all landed in the same dirty tree before anything was committed. When the operator authorised committing, **eight documents** (`architecture.md`, `deferred-work.md`, `prd.md`, `README.md`, `CHANGELOG.md`, `epics.md`, `ast_index.py`, `test_release_surface_honesty.py`) carried hunks from more than one story and could not be split without inventing hunks nobody authored. The dev applied a stated rule — *a co-edited file is carried by the LAST story that touched it* — and recorded the consequence rather than hiding it: **the four story commits are individually attributable but only the set is complete, and an intermediate commit is not expected to pass its own suite.**

That is the honest handling of the situation. It is still a real, permanent loss of bisectability across four stories, and it was avoidable by committing each story's delta as it closed.

### 3.4 🟡 Committing on the default branch, with review bypassed

The dev was delegated the branch-vs-`master` call and ruled **`master`**, on a measurement rather than a preference: the artifact being replaced *already cited `7be90f77`, a sha that is not an ancestor of this history* — it survives only on `release/epics-8-9` and on a branch literally named `backup/pre-rebase-fix`. The failure the constraint exists to prevent **has already happened once in this repository, by rebase**. Ordinary hygiene (branch before committing on the default branch) was outbid by an explicit project constraint, which is the correct application of the conflict rule, and the tradeoff was recorded rather than denied: pre-merge review was skipped; the mitigations are six small individually-messaged commits, nothing pushed, and the whole delta still facing the story's review gate.

Recorded here without a recommendation to reverse it, because the reasoning is sound and the alternative was worse. It should not silently become the default for Epic 11.

### 3.5 🟡 The register grew: 13 filed against 5 closed

Every new entry has an owner, which is the improvement Epic 9 asked for. But **nine of the thirteen carry `target_story: NONE`** with a named human and a scheduling condition instead of a story. Epic 9's §4 gave the governing insight: *a deferral addressed to a trigger executes; a deferral addressed to a condition waits forever.* Several of these are conditions (*"once Story 12.1 lifts the NFR-M1 gate"*). They are honestly recorded, better-owned than any previous cohort, and still structurally the shape that has historically not executed.

### 3.6 ⚠️ 5/5 iteration-1 PASS is a real result and must not be over-read

Epic 8 was 0-of-5 at iteration 1; Epic 9 was 1-of-2; Epic 10 is 5-of-5. Three qualifications belong beside that number, and burying them would be this project's own defect class:

1. **Two of the five stories changed zero `argus/**` code** (10.1 and 10.5 by design, DN-7). The blast radius available to a reviewer differed by story.
2. **The review model differed.** Story 10.5's gate ran on Sonnet per this project's standing convention; the others inherited the session model. A cross-epic comparison of pass rates across different reviewers measures the reviewers as much as the work.
3. **10.4 did not sail through.** It stopped on five red tests, needed an operator ruling and a fix round, and only *then* passed on review iteration 1. The story-level counter records "iteration 1" for a story that took two rounds of work.

The genuine signal underneath the number is that **the stories arrived at review already carrying re-derived evidence** — every dev re-measured the SM's figures rather than transcribing them, and the divergences (10.3's two drifted line numbers, 10.4's dogfood ratios) were reported instead of adopted.

### 3.7 🟡 The guard estate is now substantial, and nothing measures its cost

Seven new test modules this epic; `tests/test_v1_commitment_closure.py` alone is **1308 lines**. The reviewer correctly dismissed the NFR-M1 concern (the ≤1200 cap is scoped to `argus/**`, not `tests/`), and each guard earns its place. But this epic added roughly as much guard infrastructure as some earlier epics added product, several guards read **source text and walk ASTs** — deliberately, because a closure over the code is the only thing that closes the enumeration class — and a test coupled to implementation shape is a maintenance cost paid by every future story that moves a function. 10.4 mitigated it in the right way (the walk **fails loudly** if the function moves or is renamed) and this is recorded as a watch item, not a defect.

## 4. Key Insights

**1. A hand-counted enumeration in this project has been wrong on every single occasion it has been re-measured. The tally for this epic alone is five.** 10.2's amendment-site list was wrong for the third time (the "measured 10-site list" was itself incomplete *and* its line numbers had all drifted; the truth is 12 + 2 exempt). 10.5's §A.1 made it four (one named site is actually three, and one of them had been named in no planning document since 2026-08-03). 10.3's unspecified-flag count was **six**, not the ledger's four. 10.4's grammar-failure causes were **four**, not the ledger's two. And `epics.md:1932` still cites a PRD line that has moved — the fifth coordinate drift in three days, recorded by 10.5 rather than fixed, because `epics.md` was fenced. **The generalisable rule, and the successor to AI-E9-7: where an AC names a set, the load-bearing AC is a closure over the space that FAILS on an unenumerated member — the list is a convenience, never the contract.** Every story in this epic reached that conclusion independently, and in two cases the closure caught the list mid-story.

**2. A specification-correction epic cannot stay a documentation epic, and the line between "fix it" and "classify it" is one small measurement.** 10.2 could not truthfully write *"multi-language grounding is delivered in V1"* while TypeScript and PHP grounded not at all — so it fixed the loader (two data tables, measured blast radius on this repo: zero). 10.5 could not truthfully wire FR23 — every call site lands in `pipeline.py`, which is 1331/1200 and fenced — so it classified it `library-seam`, amended the FR, and stated the cost. **Both are right, and they are opposite actions.** The deciding question is not "is this a docs story" but *"is the honest amendment one small fix away from being true, or one capability away?"* Writing "8 of 10" into FR7 would have manufactured 10.5's own defect class inside the story closing its inverse — and in the *oversell* direction.

**3. Fixing a layer reveals the next layer, and the discipline is filing it, not following it.** 10.2 fixed grounding and immediately found `DF-10-2-A`: four languages now parse and extract **zero definitions**, so they cannot reach `audited_deep`. That is a real limit on what "multi-language grounding" buys a consumer, one layer below the defect just fixed, discovered because the dev's own over-strong draft assertion failed. It was pinned in both directions, disclosed **beside** the claim it qualifies (not in a footnote), and filed. The alternative — widening the definition vocabulary "while we're in here" — would have changed which files reach `audited_deep` in every polyglot repository, inside a story with no mandate over that.

**4. Two project requirements can be in mechanical contradiction, and only an implementation attempt will find it.** `AI-E8-1` says stage your new files; the dogfood staleness guards read the index. **No story can satisfy one without tripping the other.** Nine epics of story authoring never surfaced this, because no earlier story added a module to `argus/` under both regimes at once. It cost Epic 10 a HALT and an operator decision, and — see §6 — it is aimed at Epic 12 while Epic 11 is what will actually hit it.

**5. The HALT was the epic's best moment.** Given five red tests and a one-command fix (regenerate the artifacts), the dev instead stopped and wrote down that regenerating *at that moment* would stamp a provenance sha onto evidence artifacts that does not contain what they describe — *"Story 10.1's founding defect, pointed at an artifact instead of a status line."* It presented four options with a recommendation, held none of them open in code, and deliberately declined to file the systemic sub-finding on its own authority. The operator ruled, the sequence was executed in the correct order (commit → regenerate through the artifacts' **own renderers** → re-run), and every regenerated line was proven to be a **derived figure**, never an assertion. **The measure of an honesty project is what it does when the dishonest option is also the fast one.**

**6. There are three landing modes for a retrospective item, and they rank.** Epic 9 concluded that items land only when an operator converts them into acceptance criteria. Epic 10 refines that into a ranking, with evidence: **(i) a rule converted into a story-authoring standard** lands most completely — AI-E9-7 and AI-E9-8 were applied by *every story without being individually assigned*; **(ii) an item converted into a story in the plan** gets scheduled — AI-E9-4 → 11.5, AI-E9-5 → 12.1, AI-E9-6 → 11.3, all now have homes; **(iii) an item left as a runbook step for a human** is in exactly the state it was in — AI-E9-1 and AI-E9-2, unchanged, and now compounded by six unpushed commits. **The most durable output of a retrospective is not a task; it is a rule the next story author cannot avoid reading.**

**7. Committing is not evidence, and the epic said so about itself.** 10.4's fix round moved `HEAD` to a sha no CI run has ever seen and recorded: *"Committing has made that command possible, which it was not before; it has not made its answer known."* That sentence is the epic in miniature.

### 🚨 SD-1 — Epic 11 depends on "Epic 10, all five stories", and Epic 10 has never been executed by a gate
