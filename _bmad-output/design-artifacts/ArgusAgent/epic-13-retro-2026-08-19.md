## 6. Key Insights

1. **An empty result is a result, and expressing it required work nobody budgeted for.** Two
   producers refused this outcome outright, and their refusals were *correct for the world they were
   written in*. The lesson is not that the floors were wrong; it is that **a vacuity floor encodes an
   assumption about which absences are possible**, and a genuinely improved detector can create a new
   kind of absence the old floor cannot tell from breakage. 13.5's answer — narrow the floor with
   **measured evidence**, guard **both directions**, and add **no new outcome** — is the template.

2. **A guard asserting a negative must pin the precondition that makes the absence meaningful.**
   Three independent inventions of this idea in three epics, never written down. *A detector that
   never ran produces zero of both.* That sentence is the whole rule and it belongs in §Enforcement,
   registered, with its enforcing module and ids.

3. **Provenance is a property of a measurement, not of a number.** The working-tree read produced
   figures that were internally consistent, reproducible across two runs, and **labelled with a sha
   they did not come from**. `byte_reproducible_across_two_runs` was `True` the entire time. The only
   reason anyone found out is that a third party's checkout moved three times in one day. **The
   question every historical measurement in this project now owes an answer to is: which bytes did it
   read?** `DF-13-5-B` records that Epic 13's own 0 TP / 26 FP baseline came through the old
   instrument and that the answer is **not established** — and the operator ruled to record the
   uncertainty and **NOT** re-measure, which is the right call: re-running it would re-open a settled
   adjudication over a population the corrected detector no longer emits.

4. **The two highest-yield phases in this loop are the two that re-derive from the tree instead of
   reading the record**, and neither writes production code. Create-story's §0 and the review gate
   found essentially every substantive defect across two epics, one of them before implementation
   began. The transferable mechanism is not diligence; it is **independent re-derivation**.

5. **A pre-registered stopping rule is worth more than the decision it defers.** `DF-13-5-A` was
   answered before the repository was chosen, before 13.5 ran, and before any number existed. Because
   of that, this retrospective can cite it verbatim without anybody suspecting it was tuned to the
   result — and Epic 15 has a defined end even though nobody yet knows what it will find. *"ONE
   round"* is doing more work than every threshold in the protocol combined.

6. **This project can hold "blocked" as a stable terminal state without decaying into a falsehood** —
   and it has now held it twice, on two different facts, in the same vocabulary member, with the
   record saying **which** fact each time. 13.3 was `BLOCKED` on exhaustiveness; 13.5 is `BLOCKED` on
   the denominator. Most projects cannot stop here without something starting to lie.

7. **A closed schema and an append-only record buy correctness at the cost of readability, and the
   cost is real.** The adjudication record's header still says no adjudication has taken place; the
   ledger's honest half-closures defeat its own analyzer. **Both are the append-only property working
   as designed**, and both are traps for a reader who takes a summary field at face value. The remedy
   is never to rewrite — it is to make the derived surface, not the header, the thing anybody reads.
