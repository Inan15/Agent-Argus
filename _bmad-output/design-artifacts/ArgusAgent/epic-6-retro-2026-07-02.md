## 4. Key Insights

- **The adversarial gate's highest value is catching a keystone-property violation that the AUTHOR'S OWN TESTS structurally cannot see — and Epic 6 is the cleanest proof of that yet.** The 6.7 append-only collision was invisible to a test suite that (reasonably, by every prior convention) varied its fixtures. Only an independent reviewer who asked "what if two events are byte-identical?" — the exact AC4 case — found it. This validates the whole "fresh context, different-LLM, independently-reproduce, don't-trust-the-Dev-record" review posture as the load-bearing defense for governance-critical properties, and it argues for a specific new fixture discipline (below).

- **Content-addressed / hash-keyed storage has a latent identity-collapse hazard that is now a KNOWN recurring shape, not a one-off.** 6.7's collision is the SECOND time the "two distinct things can produce byte-identical bytes" hazard has bitten near the store layer (the Epic-5 retro's 5.2 `test_lookup_containment_traversal_key_is_a_miss` name-vs-behavior mismatch was a milder cousin — a docstring claiming a MISS where the code raises). Any place APAA derives a filename or a chain link from `hash(payload)`, the author must ask "can two logically-distinct events collide here, and is that intended?" — for the cache (5.2) collision IS the point (dedup), for the decision chain (6.7) it is a corruption. This is a distinct-enough, twice-relevant shape to earn a create-story/code-review checklist line (§9.2 rule-of-three heuristic: this is occurrence 2 of the identity-collapse shape near the store layer, and the cost of the checklist line is trivial vs one more governance-chain corruption slipping through).

- **The complete-the-declared-set discipline now operates at a FOURTH level: payload/event IDENTITY, on top of fixture coverage-classes (AI-E3-1), no-crash input-shapes (AI-E4-1), and closure/enumeration completeness (5.1).** The unifying failure mode remains "an enumeration that omits a member," and Epic 6 revealed the member can be a *dimension of variation the fixtures held constant* (here: payload identity). The durable corrective stays the same — enumerate the full declared set and cover each member — but the "declared set" must now be read to include "the identity/collision dimension of anything hash-keyed."

- **Sequencing a dated carry-forward to a specific owning story ONE EPIC OUT works — DF-1-7-B is the proof.** The Epic-5 retro's marquee recommendation (treat 6.2 as a hard deliverable that closes DF-1-7-B before the dogfood) was followed and the four-retro-old debt is retired. The same technique should now be applied to DF-6-6-A: it must be treated as a hard Epic-7 pre-condition (with an explicit non-code adjudication + corpus-growth sub-task), not a background 🟠 defer, or the dogfood's externalization claim rests on an un-cleared gate.

- **Defer-inflow rising is not automatically a warning when the epic OPENS a new capability surface — the character of the defers matters more than the count.** Epic 6's 5-filed / 1-closed inflow would read as regrowth by a raw count, but four are scoped V2-boundary lines the design deliberately drew and one is an honest gate-provisionality note. The §9.3 metric should be read alongside a severity + category breakdown, not as a bare integer. The healthier lead indicator for Epic 7 is: does the ONE 🟠 (DF-6-6-A) close, and do the four 🟢 V2 items get PACKAGED (into a V2 resolved-graph epic) rather than scattered.
