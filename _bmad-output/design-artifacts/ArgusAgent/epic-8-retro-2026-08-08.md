## 2. What Went Well (Successes)

1. **The epic did to itself what it demands of others — and the review gate proved it rather than accepting it.** Every load-bearing claim in Epic 8 was re-derived by someone other than its author. 8.1's reviewer stood up a **clean detached worktree at `9109e16`** to confirm which reds were inherited (and found the story's own blast-radius table wrong — `test_dogfood_proof.py` was green at baseline, so its failure was delta-caused). 8.2's reviewer **differentially compared the restructured `is_test_file` against HEAD's body rebuilt from `git show` over 220 paths × 12 entry variants = 2 567 comparisons → 0 mismatches**, proving the restructure was a pure extraction. 8.4's reviewer **executed HEAD's `_assurance_statement` and HEAD's fold** to check every "before" string in the changelog. 8.5's reviewer **re-rendered all three artifacts and sha256-compared them**. Not one of these is a reading of the Dev Agent Record. On an epic whose entire subject is *"do not assert what you did not measure"*, the gate held the deliverable to the deliverable's own standard.

2. **Runtime injection of the historical implementation became this project's standard proof that a regression test is real — and it is the single most valuable process asset Epic 8 produced.** Invented in 8.2 (a pytest plugin rebinding `argus.pipeline._critical_ineligibility` to the pre-fix body, *and* to the **forbidden naive hoist**, showing `-002-09` discriminates the correct fix from the wrong one **in both directions**), it was then reused in 8.3 (recompiling the round-1 `generator.render_final_verdict_report` and the historic `plain_english._headline`), in 8.4 (injecting the pre-fix `argus/cli.py` to watch `TC-ArgusAgent-CLI-001-33` go **RED with a real uncaught `ShipReadinessError`**, while `-30`/`-31`/`-32` stayed green — the proof that `-32` never exercised the site), and in 8.5 (running `_DIR_TOKEN` over the three **HEAD** artifacts to see it flag `minions_core/` citations the old suffix-only pattern was structurally blind to). This converts *"the dev says the test was RED-first"* into *"the reviewer watched it go red against the real pre-fix code."* **No source file is edited to do it.**

3. **The story-ordering constraint worked exactly as designed — the deliberate visible slip was visible, owned, and closed.** The epic put 8.5 last *"so a slip is visible"*. 8.1's decision-table reorder immediately turned `test_dogfood_proof.py::test_committed_proof_artifact_exists_and_matches_live_run` red, because the committed proof said `NOT_READY_FOR_RELEASE` / exit `2` while the live re-derivation said otherwise. ## 3. Challenges & Growth Areas

1. **The dominant defect class of this epic was *the guard is narrower than the AC it implements* — and it appeared in all five stories.** This is the finding worth carrying forward above every other:
   - **8.1 / R2** — the FR19 "never upgrades" clause was asserted over a **private rank map**, not through `prosecute()`; the branch that actually enforces it *"is never executed by any test"*, and the test's own docstring claimed the case *"cannot be manufactured"* — **which was false, and the reviewer manufactured it.**
   - **8.2** — the AC3 fold test was **tautological**: it handed `identify_critical_subsystems` a candidate with `ineligibility=None` and asserted it survived, restating the fold rule while the property under test lived entirely in the **shell** the test never exercised. *"Consequence: the only real D3 pin covers only the unambiguously-named parse-failed file — which is precisely why the High went undetected."*
   - **8.3** — AC11's cross-surface net checked block-assertion consistency but never that a `Next:` **pointer resolved**, so the report could tell an operator *"see the final-verdict report for the named critical files"* while that document contained **zero occurrences of the word "critical"**.
   - **8.4** — the worst case, because the defeated guard **was the rot check written to prevent exactly this**: reverting `plain_english._headline`'s row-4 wording left **all 19 rot-check tests green** (the note publishes that same text as history, and `-05` was a whole-document substring search); the `1/5` floor is published **six times and pinned by nothing**; two of row 4's **three** callout shapes were **structurally unpublishable** by the test's own duplicate-detection; and `-02` could not detect a schema **downgrade** because it scanned the whole table row including the *Before* cell.
   - **8.5 / F2** — `_PATH_TOKEN` matched only tokens ending in a file suffix, so **directory citations were outside the guard AC11b demanded** — which is how `excluding \`argus/tests/\`` (a directory that does not exist) reached three published artifacts.

   Five stories, five instances. The pattern is not carelessness; it is that **an AC saying "every X" was discharged by a test sampling *one* X**. The counter-discipline is visible in the fixes — enumerate the space (`(row, variant)` keys, `_REPORT_POINTERS` with a **fail-on-unregistered** rule, per-row `==` comparison instead of substring search) — and it should be a story-authoring rule, not a review rescue.

## 4. Key Insights

- **Self-checking has a floor, and Epic 8 located it exactly: you re-derive what you think to re-derive.** Every one of the four "asserted the unmeasured" escapes (§3.2) was a figure the author *believed was already established* — copied from their own story context, or inherited from a note written minutes earlier. The dev discipline on this project is genuinely high (RED-first captured verbatim, variances recorded, strikethrough not deletion), and it still cannot catch an inherited belief, because the check and the belief share an origin. **Only an independent party re-executing the producing code catches it.** This is the strongest argument in the whole project's history for the adversarial-review posture, and it was made by the epic least likely to need it.

- **A guard written against a *known* defect protects against that defect; a guard written against a *space* protects against the next one.** The recurring §3.1 pattern has one shape: an AC says "every X", and the test asserts one X. The fixes that closed it all did the same thing — enumerate the space and **fail on an unregistered member** (`_REPORT_POINTERS` failing an unregistered `Next:` pointer; `_published_callouts` keyed on `(row, variant)` over three real folds; `_DIR_TOKEN` widened generically with `argus/tests/` explicitly **not** special-cased, on the reviewer's instruction: *"Do not close this by special-casing"*). The generalisable rule: **when an AC quantifies universally, the test must enumerate and reject the unenumerated.**

- **A ceiling is a tripwire, not a design.** NFR-M1's 1200-line fence did exactly what a tripwire does — it stopped 8.2 adding a helper to `pipeline.py` and stopped 8.5 extracting a renderer from `proof_run.py`, and in both cases the *placement* decision it forced was independently correct (8.2's substance went into `vacuous_test.py`, the module that **owns** the classification — AR7 no-fork). What it did not do is cause the split. Two modules now sit at 1199 and 1196 with the next largest at 705, and the split is a **precondition** of four ledger items rather than a cleanup. The lesson is not to raise the fence; it is that **a tripwire needs an owner and a trigger threshold below the wire** — "the first story after a module passes 1100 lines owns its split" would have cost one story in Epic 6 and now costs an unplanned refactor inside a release epic.

- **The repo separation quietly changed the *class* of Argus's flagship evidence, and only 8.5's added AC2 kept that honest.** The dogfood generator's prose said Minions; its code has defaulted to `scope_prefix="argus"` since the separation. Regenerating it would have republished a Minions claim over Argus bytes. Measured, the substitution is stark: **2906 findings over 135 modules / 36712 LOC (independent) → 101 findings over 69 modules / 18276 LOC (self-audit)**; `cross_partition` 332→2, `hardcoded_secret` 2289→22, `orphan_code` 285→77. The re-derived artifact says so in its own §1 and the ledger progress note says so again. **A structural change to a repository can silently invalidate the subject of an artifact without touching a line of that artifact's generator** — and the only thing that caught it was an SM reading the code instead of the prose while contexting the story.

- **"File it" is only honest when the filing is audited.** This epic's ledger discipline is excellent *because* reviewers repeatedly checked whether the claimed coverage existed: 8.5/F1 falsified *"this is covered by DF-6-6-A-P1 / DF-7-2-A"* by reading `deferred-work.md:362-394`; 8.5/F5 falsified *"DF-8-2-A establishes the precedent so no new id is needed"* with *"`DF-8-2-A` is the precedent for **filing an id**, not a substitute for one"*, and the resulting `DF-8-5-D` is **better than the review asked for** (it carries a corrected blocker and a constraint the reviewer did not have). Left unaudited, the same two sentences would have been indistinguishable from coverage.

- **The most reliable predictor of a story passing review here is whether its evidence was captured *before* the source changed.** 8.1's AC11 fixture — the actual persisted `.argus/` bytes of a pre-amendment run, captured before touching any source — is the single cleanest piece of evidence in the epic, and the reviewer verified it by **regenerating it inside the `9109e16` worktree** and finding all 10 artifact bodies byte-identical. Contrast 8.4's R1, where the "before" string was reconstructed *after the fact* from a context table and was wrong. **Capture the baseline first; a reconstructed baseline is a claim, not a measurement.**
